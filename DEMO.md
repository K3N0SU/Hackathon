# Demo plan for Furrow

## Before you record or present

Open the live site on your phone, not the laptop. Set the phone language to English, clear the site's storage so no old clocks show, turn the volume up, and turn on Do Not Disturb. Have the Valley Guard label open full screen on the laptop or printed, so the camera has something real to point at. Check the phone has a Punjabi voice: tap Listen in Punjabi once. If it says no voice is installed, use Spanish for the audio moment and keep Punjabi for the text.

Run the full path twice in a row before you start. If the live scan is slow, the sample label falls back to a saved answer by itself.

## The 75 second story

Start on the label, before the app. Hold up the printed Valley Guard label. Say: this is what a worker gets before walking into an almond orchard. Small English print. Many of the people working these rows in San Joaquin County read Spanish or Punjabi first.

Open the site on the phone and scroll the landing page for a second, then tap Open Furrow, switch to Punjabi, tap Scan, take the photo. While it reads (about five seconds), say: Gemini is reading the photo and returning a fixed set of fields, not a paragraph.

When the result appears, point at the green box: twelve hours, in Punjabi. Tap Listen if the voice works. Scroll to the gear and first aid. Point at the Call Poison Control button.

Tap Start the clock, leave the time as now, and go back to the start screen. Show the red countdown. Say: reading it once isn't enough, so Furrow keeps the clock, and it survives a reload. Reload the page to prove it.

Close with the safety rule. Take a photo of something that is not a label, like your hand or the table. Show that it says it can't read it and doesn't guess. Say: when it can't tell, it says so.

Last line: Furrow. Know before you walk in.

## What to say about the rubric without saying the rubric

Innovation: a countdown that outlives the scan, and a tool that refuses to guess. Technical: schema-constrained output from Gemini, client-side image downscale, a fallback for the demo labels, speech synthesis, all on free tiers. Impact: Spanish and Punjabi readers in San Joaquin County, a real hazard, a real phone. Ease of use: one button, no account.

## If the judges ask

Is it accurate? Not yet certified. The translations are machine translations and need review by native speakers and people in pesticide safety before anyone relies on it, and it says so on screen. Why Punjabi? Punjabi speakers are a large part of the farm community in this region and almost nothing is written for them. What happens with no signal? The three sample labels fall back to saved answers; a fully offline installed version is the next step. What does it cost? Nothing to run, it is all on free tiers.

## Demo video (60 to 90 seconds, screen recording)

Record the phone screen with the built-in screen recorder and speak over it, or record the laptop with the site open at phone width. Follow the 75 second story above in the same order. Start on the printed label, end on the line Know before you walk in. Do one clean take, trim the start and end, and keep the audio clear. Upload it to YouTube as unlisted and paste the link into Devpost.
